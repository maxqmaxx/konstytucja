# World constitutions: a workshop for thinking about a better (Polish) constitution

Open data and worked examples for anyone discussing constitutional design, with a focus on Poland:

- prevalence of 331 constitutional features (Comparative Constitutions Project topic coding) across 188 constitutions in force, also for constitutions adopted since 2000 and for the EU-27;
- the Polish Constitution of 1997, article by article, against that background, with manually reviewed gaps;
- 53 745 foreign provisions with a Polish one-line summary, normative strength and debate-problem tags (machine-generated, Claude Opus 5.5, closed vocabularies), each linked to the full text on Constitute;
- worked examples, 24 proposals for Poland, and the code to reproduce everything.

**Separation of powers in one line:** the President of Poland holds 16.3% of decision power across 11 key decisions (world median for heads of state: 29.2%); the Sejm overrides his veto with 3/5, so 40% of deputies sustain it, a threshold only 3 constitutions share. Report (Polish): [analizy/podzial_wladzy.md](analizy/podzial_wladzy.md).

**Browse it online: [maxqmaxx.github.io/konstytucja](https://maxqmaxx.github.io/konstytucja/)** (Polish interface): the Polish Constitution article by article against the world; 23 debate problems leading to foreign solutions; 331 topic pages with filters (EU only, post-2000, normative strength, variant); exact variants for 49 key design choices (term limits, law-making and second chambers, government formation and no-confidence, countersignature, budget, veto override, amendment, emergencies, constitutional court access, judicial council, judge removal, immunity, central bank, referendum, land sale bans, debt limits, inalienable resources, treaties, war powers, conscription, health, environment, privacy, parties, electoral bodies…); a constitution builder (pick your own set and see the closest constitutions, differences from Poland and known tensions); statistics on length and popularity of solutions; a separation-of-powers model (who decides and who blocks: Shapley-Shubik power shares of chambers, head of state, government, the people and courts in 11 key decisions, veto players and blocking minorities, for all 188 constitutions); a world map by feature or year of adoption; side-by-side comparison of 2–3 constitutions; 24 proposals with risks and safeguards; an atlas of 130 unusual provisions; country pages; and a personal folder ("teczka") to collect provisions and export them to Word, Markdown or PDF with citations (stored only in your browser). Every record has a "report an error" link that opens a prefilled GitHub issue; light and dark theme.

Constitution texts and translations by third parties are not redistributed; see [docs/czego-nie-ma.md](docs/czego-nie-ma.md). Code: MIT. Own content: CC BY 4.0. Data as of 2026-11-07. Most documentation is in Polish.
